The AI harness that knows journalism.

The problem

Every investigation starts in the noise.

Tables, documents, recordings, agency copy. One reporter, a deadline, and nobody to think it through with.

AI has made it cheap to turn recordings and documents into text, summaries and candidate facts. It has not made it any cheaper to check them. In many newsrooms AI arrives in separate pieces: transcription in one tool, a chatbot in another, a verification plug-in somewhere else, with copy and paste in between and no record of where a statement came from.

Checking is the bottleneck. Open Journalist does not promise to save you time. It aims for more verified statements in the time you have.

The idea

From material to a story with evidence.

One place for the whole editorial process, from the first file to the signed-off draft. Not another tool in the chain, but the harness around it.

01

You bring material.

Everything you have: documents, tables, scans, recordings, links. You only say how confidential it is.

02

Open Journalist sorts it.

It analyzes data locally, proposes theses and looks for the evidence behind them, down to the line and the stored query.

03

You decide.

What holds becomes your story, in the formats you need. What does not hold stays visible as a contradiction or a gap.

Live · the demo case “E-Auto-Check”

What is in the numbers?

A short text claims that Germany registered 545,000 new electric cars in 2025, and that an analysis on the web counts 1.6 million. Next to it: a table of new registrations from 2013 to October 2026, about 60,000 rows. Here is what Open Journalist does with it.

1

“Give me what you have.”

A project starts with one field, not a form. Drop in files, paste links, type or speak what the story is about. Nothing is opened until you have said how confidential each file is: public, internal, confidential or source protection.

2

Explore: the data speaks first.

The table is analyzed on your computer, without a model. The first finding is the one that matters: the file mixes single rows with subtotals and grand totals. Summing every row would count the same cars 2.99 times.

Every value comes from a stored query that you can open and run again. Computed values are marked as such: they are not in the original.

3

Theses, not answers.

Only after you say “yes, send it” does the model see the approved material. It proposes theses, each with what carries it. A thesis is a proposal, drawn dashed, until you choose it.

4

Check: one claim holds, one does not.

Each claim is pinned to its line in the text. Figures are checked against the data with a stored query, computed by the app, never by the model.

545,142
electric cars in 2025, from the 12 grand-total rows. Supports “around 545,000”.
1.6 million
contradicted: 1,631,168 is what you get when model rows, subtotals and totals are summed together.
5

Build: one story, every channel.

From the confirmed claims, the Story Builder drafts a web article and a radio two-way, each in its format template. Every sentence carries the claims it rests on. Sentences whose evidence did not hold are dropped, and the draft says so.

Format templates are plain Markdown. A newsroom adds its own formats without writing code.

6

Release: people sign off.

Release is blocked until the required quality gates are decided by a person: G03 source and evidence check and G14 sign-off and release. Then the story goes out as Markdown, HTML, Word or as a draft for your content management system, with its list of sources and their hashes.

The principle

The machine proposes. You decide.

Every statement shows what it rests on. What is missing stays visible as a gap and is never filled with generated text.

Proposed

A thesis of the agent, still open.

Supported

Evidence in the data or the material.

Contradicted

Does not hold up to the check.

Gap

No evidence. Stays visibly open.

Released

Signed off by you.

What makes it different

A harness for journalism, not another chatbot.

General-purpose AI tools optimize for answers. Journalism needs answers it can defend. Open Journalist is built around the rules of the newsroom, and enforces them in code.

Evidence binding

Every statement shows its evidence.

Each claim points to the exact passage, line, page or second of a recording it came from. Figures come from stored queries on approved tables. A statement without evidence is shown as a gap.

Human sign-off

The agent proposes, people decide.

Anything the agent adds stays a proposal until a person confirms it. Eighteen quality gates, from the source check to legal review, block release until the responsible person has decided them.

Source protection

Your material stays yours.

Everything lives on your machine, in open formats. The sensitivity of the material decides which model may see it, and source-protected material never goes to a cloud service. No telemetry, no crash reports.

Model-agnostic

One core, from one person to a network.

The same core serves a freelancer, a small team and a media organization. It works with local models, your organization's own inference or a provider you choose. Tools connect through open standards such as MCP.

What it does not promise

It is not a truth machine, it does not publish on its own, and it does not replace your content management system, your archive or your agency.

How it works

Six steps on one thread.

The thread is dashed until a person confirms, and solid after.

  1. 1

    Bring in

    Documents, scans, recordings and data go into the story's store, with their origin and a sensitivity class you set.

  2. 2

    Open up

    Transcription and text recognition run locally. Data is queried read-only, after you approve it.

  3. 3

    Agent proposes

    Events, people, organizations and claims, each pinned to its passage. Patterns become leads, not findings.

  4. 4

    You confirm

    Check each proposal side by side with its evidence. What you confirm forms the story graph.

  5. 5

    Build

    Drafts in several forms, from web to radio to print, all from the same confirmed claims.

  6. 6

    Sign off

    The required gates are passed, then the story goes to your CMS as a draft, with its evidence record.

Sensitivity routing

Confidentiality is enforced, not hoped for.

Every file carries a sensitivity class. Every model endpoint has a list of classes it may receive. A request that would send material to an endpoint that is not allowed to see it is refused before it leaves your computer, and the refusal is recorded.

Under the hood

The story graph.

At the heart of Open Journalist is the story graph: events are its nodes, and the relations between them (follows, causes, updates, contradicts) are its edges. Claims, sources and evidence hang on the events.

It is the journalistic substrate of a story: what research distills from raw material. From its confirmed part you build the story in any form, today a text and a radio script, tomorrow a format nobody has asked for yet.

The idea comes from structured journalism and from the working group “Unit of News” at the AI for Media Network's symposium in Tutzing, 2024.

How it grows

Built in the open, for every newsroom.

Journalism needs AI tools it can inspect, run on its own terms and adapt. A shared foundation that freelancers, small teams, public broadcasters and publishers can all use and improve.

Formats as text

Format templates and story recipes are Markdown files. A new format or a house rule needs no code.

Your model

The endpoint is a setting: operated sovereignly, at a European provider or locally. Nothing is hard-coded.

Source protection

Source-protected material goes only to a model on your own computer. Keys live in the operating system's keychain.

Open licence

European Union Public Licence 1.2. The project can later be handed to a neutral operating body.

Potential and scale

From the kitchen table to the newsroom.

  3. 2027, second half

    Teams and whole newsrooms

    Roles, team repositories, shared inference, connection to archive and CMS.

  4. 2028

    A network of newsrooms

    A Trusted Content Pool across organizations: signed attestations, never raw material.

Calendar targets are orientation, not commitments.

Where it comes from

Robin's concept, built in 46 hours.

Open Journalist took shape in the AI for Media Network, a network of AI practitioners from media organizations in Germany, Austria and Switzerland. The idea and the first product concept come from Robin Greene. At the AI for Media Hackathon 2026 in Munich, a team of six people and their agents turned it into a working prototype.

Open Journalist is a personal initiative, not a project of any broadcaster or publisher.
